A comet close enough to the Sun for its nucleus to be actively shedding material. Two tails are visible and they are produced differently: the broad, slightly curved tail is dust released from the nucleus and pushed outward by radiation pressure, while the narrow bluish streak is the ion tail, gas ionised by ultraviolet sunlight and swept straight back by the solar wind. Because the two respond to different forces, they rarely point in exactly the same direction.
